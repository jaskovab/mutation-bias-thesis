# The Influence of Mutation Bias on Adaptive Evolution
## Bachelor thesis

### Abstract
In evolutionary biology, natural selection acts upon genetic variation, which arises through mutations. However, mutations do not happen with equal probability, their occurrence is shaped by mutation biases; for example, a general tendency to favour transitions over transversions, or to prefer substitutions towards AT rather than GC. Recent studies clearly document that such biases are not overridden by natural selection but can influence the direction of adaptive evolution. Studies explored in this thesis have shown that the spectrum of adaptive substitutions can be predicted from the mutation spectrum, and even vice versa. Many underlying biases however remain unknown or unexplored. In this thesis, we investigate whether the structure of the genetic code introduces a mutation bias that influences the frequency of adaptive substitutions. We hypothesised that changes accessible through a greater number of single-nucleotide substitutions would occur more frequently among adaptive mutations. To test this, we applied correlation analyses and a negative binomial model to a dataset of antibiotics resistance-conferring substitutions in *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. The analysis did not reveal a significant relationship, potentially due to disruptive factors in the data, such as their skewed distribution or other mutation biases.
